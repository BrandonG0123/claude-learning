Twelve rounds of review

Each round I watched it as someone seeing it for the first time, wrote down what they'd notice, fixed it, and watched again. The full log is review-log.md.

  1. Round 1

    A wall of yellow, a dull flight, a whipping camera. The first frame was a screen-filling lime macro; key and rim came from the same side; the ball jumped across the frame; the burn edge was a thick orange worm.

  2. Round 2

    Relit, one camera move, fibres, dust, a rubber core. The camera became one eased move, checked numerically frame by frame. Burnt felt now shows charred rubber, not a hollow shell.

  3. Round 3

    Tuning. The rim light blew out to neon and the cyan turned the felt green; smoke smeared the ball; the char read as rust. Each fixed, the felt checked with the fire switched off.

  4. Round 4

    The opening goes dark. Only the rim catches the fuzz at first; the light comes up as the camera pulls back. The fire now dies with the ball; the ignition flares; the fibres crimp.

  5. Round 5

    The whole page together. Found and fixed a double exposure where the film faded in over its own poster. The staircase was a pile of shards; it's now a spiral staircase with a handrail that fills with light.

  6. Round 6

    Using it. Skip and Sound moved ahead of the thirty links for keyboards and screen readers; the fallback tiles laid out properly; the phone's item list marked; the sound raised from −32 to −26 LUFS.

  7. Round 7

    Honesty and the payoff. The burning ball is now labelled "Illustration · rendered, not filmed" while it's on screen. The climax gained a ripple out to the ring.

  8. Round 8

    Ten screen sizes. The header wrapped on iPads; on small phones the open list hit the buttons and the label hit a piece. All fixed; the ring now fits itself to short phones.

  9. Round 9

    The final film. Rendered at full quality in both cuts and re-recorded with the page; the hand-off and the first frame checked again at full size. The film now shows only once it's actually playing, because Safari can paint an empty video black over the poster.

  10. Round 10

    The live path, checked like the rest. axe on the moving version too (it normally only sees the stills): no violations. Reading mode keeps the stage dark like a screen in the page. Big screens now keep the canvas within a pixel budget, so laptops stay smooth.

  11. Round 11

    Skip, Replay and sound, traced against the score. Skip lands just before the title with everything printed and no stray sounds; Replay brings the film and the credit back and remembers a mute. Nothing to change.

  12. Round 12

    Final checks. The full suite on the final build, Lighthouse on every page (About 100 / 100 / 100), and the phone recorded again with its finished tall cut.

What I couldn't do from here

  • Play it live. There's no graphics card here, so I've never seen it run in real time. Please watch it on your Mac and on your phone first.
  • Test in Safari or on the iPhone simulator; this cloud machine has neither.
  • Hear it. I judged the sound from loudness measurements and a spectrogram, not by ear.
  • Fetch free library sounds. The cloud environment's network policy blocks kenney.nl and freesound.org; allowing them in its settings would let me add CC0 effects, each recorded in docs/licences.md.
  • Render the film with Remotion. In software it takes about two minutes a frame, so I rendered the same scene code with a lighter script. On your Mac, npm run render:film re-renders it in minutes.
  • Screen-reader testing beyond the accessibility tree and the tab order.
